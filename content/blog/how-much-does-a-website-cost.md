# How Much Does a Website Cost?

Website pricing sounds mysterious because people make it mysterious.

It is not. The price changes because the job changes.

A page that only exists is cheap. A site that makes customers trust you costs more. A site that processes orders, handles operations, and supports growth costs even more.

I built 15 live demos so you can see the difference instead of trusting a pricing table.

## Quick Answer

A website can cost anywhere from $20 to $10,000+.

The honest version is this: under $200 is usually a compromise, $200-$500 is where a real business website starts, $500+ is where the site starts helping you compete, and $1,000+ is where it starts doing actual business work.

## Website Cost by Budget

| Budget | What It Usually Means | Honest Verdict |
|--------|------------------------|----------------|
| $20 - $100 | Cheap placeholder | Fine for hobbies, bad for real businesses |

| $1,000 - $2,000 | Business system | The site starts doing work for you |
| $2,000 - $5,000 | Growth-stage build | More locations, integrations, and complexity |
| $5,000 - $10,000+ | Custom platform | When the website is part of operations |

## What Should You Budget?

If you are a small local business, start around $200-$500. That gets you out of the danger zone where the website hurts trust.

If you are a restaurant that needs online ordering or reservations, think closer to $500-$1,000.

If you are selling products online, start at $500 and go up depending on products, payments, inventory, and operations.

If the website is part of how the business runs, not just how it markets itself, then $1,000+ starts making sense.

## FAQ

### How much does a restaurant website cost?

Most independent restaurants should expect $200-$2,000. A simple professional presence can sit around $200-$500. Ordering, reservations, and better flows push it higher.

### How much does an ecommerce website cost?

Usually $500 and up. Below that, you often get something that looks like a store but still depends on manual work behind the scenes.

### What is the cheapest way to get a website?

If you truly have no budget, start with a Google Business Profile. It will usually help a local business more than a bad website.

### Why do some websites cost $10,000?

Because at that level you are not paying for a few pages. You are paying for custom software, integrations, performance, workflows, and something the business can rely on.

## The Bottom Line

Most people do not need a $10,000 website.

But a lot of businesses need more than a $20 or $100 website.

That middle range is where the real decision is. Spend enough that the website helps you instead of making customers doubt you.
