# What a $100 Website Actually Gets You

A $100 website is not the same disaster as a $20 website. That is why it is more dangerous.

It looks close enough to real that people start expecting it to behave like a real business tool. But most of the time, it is still just a nicer placeholder.

## What Works

To be fair, a $100 website can work.

The products show up. The menu is visible. The contact information is there. The homepage does not look completely broken. If you just need a link for your Instagram bio, it can do that job.

This is not garbage. It is just limited.

That distinction matters because some small businesses really do operate this way. The customer browses, messages you on WhatsApp, asks questions, sends payment, and you handle the rest manually.

That model can work if you chose it on purpose.

The problem is that a $100 website usually forces you into that model whether you chose it or not.

## Where It Breaks

The website does not take work away from you. It sends the work back to you.

Orders still happen in messages. Inventory still lives in your head or in a spreadsheet. Payments still need manual follow-up. Every order becomes a conversation.

That is fine when you have three orders a week. It becomes a mess when you have twenty orders a day.

The site also looks forgettable. It is usually a template with some text changed. Your customer may not hate it, but they probably will not remember it either.

And performance is still a problem. Slow pages, heavy images, and awkward mobile behavior are common at this level. It is better than the $20 version, but better does not mean good.

## The Honest Verdict

A $100 website gives you online existence. It does not give you leverage.

It works if you are testing a business idea, selling low volume, or intentionally using WhatsApp as your order system.

It does not work if you want customers to find you through Google, if you want orders to process without you, or if you are trying to look established next to competitors with real websites.

Do not expect ROI from a $100 website. At best, it is a digital business card. Somewhere to send people who already know about you.

## The Upgrade Signal

The signal is simple: when you spend more than two hours a week managing orders through WhatsApp, the cheap website is no longer cheap.

That time is not free. It is time you could spend making the product, serving customers, improving operations, or getting new business.

Other signs are easy to spot.

- You are embarrassed to send the link
- Customers complain that the site is slow
- You lose track of orders
- You keep answering the same questions manually
- You know the site exists but it does not really help
