# Why Not to Buy Cheap Websites

Someone offers you a business website for $20. It sounds like a steal until you remember that the website is not there to exist. It is there to make strangers trust you.

And this is where the cheap website starts costing you.

## Try the Demo First

I built the bad version on purpose so this is not just theory.

- [$20 Restaurant Website](https://20-dollar-restaurant.vercel.app)
- [$20 Ecommerce Store](https://20-dollar-ecommerce-app.vercel.app)

Open them on your phone. Wait for them to load. Try to find the menu. Try to contact the business. Try to buy something.

Done? Good. That uncomfortable feeling is the whole point.

## What $20 Actually Buys

At this budget you are not buying a real website. You are buying a page that technically exists.

You get slow loading. You get placeholder text. You get buttons that look clickable but do nothing. You get a mobile layout that feels like nobody opened it on a phone before shipping it.

The worst part is not that it looks cheap. The worst part is that it makes the business look suspicious.

When a stranger lands on your website, they are asking one question: is this a real business?

A $20 website usually answers no.

## The Trust Problem

People do not need a perfect website to trust you. But they do need signs that someone cared.

If the site takes forever to load, they leave. If the mobile layout is broken, they leave. If the contact button does nothing, they leave. They do not write you a report explaining why. They just go back to Google or Instagram and click the next option.

That is the real cost of a cheap website. It is not the $20. It is the customers you never know you lost.

## You Will Probably Pay Twice

This is the trap.

You buy the cheap site because you want to save money. Then it does not work. Then customers complain, or worse, nobody contacts you at all. Then you finally pay for the real website you should have built first.

So the $20 website did not save you money. It added a useless step before the real expense.

## When a $20 Website Makes Sense

There are a few honest cases where it is fine.

- You are testing an idea and do not know if the business will exist next month
- You are a hobbyist and not trying to look professional
- You have literally no budget and need a temporary page today

That is it.

If you are asking strangers to trust you with their time, money, food order, booking, or card details, a $20 website is not a shortcut. It is a warning sign.

## If You Have No Budget, Do This Instead

Use a Google Business Profile.

It is free. It shows your hours, photos, reviews, map location, and phone number. For a local business, that is usually more useful than a broken cheap website.

A clean Google profile with real reviews beats a bad website almost every time.

## The Bottom Line

A cheap website is not bad because it is cheap. It is bad because it makes the customer do extra work to trust you.

And most customers will not do that work.

If the website makes you look less real than a Google Business Profile, do not buy it.
